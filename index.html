  • Why Earth is tilted

  • Why the sky is blue

    Rayleigh scattering makes air molecules deflect short wavelengths far more than long ones, so the sky is scattered sunlight with the blue left in. The fourth-power law also explains the violet problem and the pale horizon.

    Clojure, OCaml, Erlang and Rust make immutability the default for reasons of concurrency and reasoning, then pay for it in allocation, asymptotic behaviour and escape hatches such as transients and mutable fields.

  • Why TCP needs a three-way handshake instead of two

    A two-message handshake would confirm only one direction of initial sequence numbers and could leave a server holding state for a connection the client never opened. The third message completes both confirmations and gives old duplicate SYNs a way to be rejected.

  • What a write-ahead log solves, and what it costs

    A write-ahead log replaces a scatter of page flushes at commit time with one sequential append and one flush, which is why commits survive power cuts. The price is duplicated writes, a flush on the commit path, and checkpointing that costs disk space and recovery time.

  • B-tree vs binary search tree: where the difference actually shows up

    A B-tree puts many keys and children in each node while a binary search tree puts one, which cuts the number of block reads needed for a million keys from about twenty to three and shifts insert cost, memory use and range scans.
